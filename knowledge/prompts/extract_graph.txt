-Goal-
Build an evidence-backed knowledge graph from software, SaaS, cloud, licensing,
support, privacy, security, and data-processing agreements.

The input may contain metadata markers such as [DOCUMENT], [CLAUSE_ID],
[SECTION], [SECTION_PATH], [SCOPE], and [CLAUSE_TEXT]. Preserve those markers
when describing evidence and scope.

-Instructions-
1. Identify entities of these types: [{entity_types}].
   Important entities include documents, clauses, definitions, parties, roles,
   services, software, licence models, rights, obligations, prohibitions,
   permissions, exceptions, conditions, remedies, data categories, SLA metrics,
   fees, dates, territories, and governing-law concepts.

2. Treat a material contractual rule as an entity when needed to preserve its
   complete meaning. Its description must state:
   - actor;
   - modality (MAY, MUST, MUST NOT, or IS DEFINED AS);
   - action and object;
   - product/document scope;
   - conditions and exceptions;
   - source document and section.

3. Identify relationships only when the source text supports them. Start each
   relationship description with the most appropriate relation label:
   DEFINES, APPLIES_TO, GRANTS, REQUIRES, PROHIBITS, PERMITS, CONDITIONED_ON,
   EXCEPTION_TO, QUALIFIES, OVERRIDES, AMENDS, SUPERSEDES,
   INCORPORATES_BY_REFERENCE, REMEDY_FOR, SURVIVES_TERMINATION, or SUPPORTED_BY.

4. Never collapse a general rule and a product-specific rule into one
   unconditional statement. Preserve negation, "unless", "except",
   "notwithstanding", and "provided that". Do not infer that one document
   overrides another unless the text supports that conclusion.

5. Every entity and relationship description must include the best available
   [SECTION] and [SCOPE]. Do not invent missing terms.

-Output format-
For each entity:
("entity"<|><entity_name><|><entity_type><|><entity_description>)

For each relationship:
("relationship"<|><source_entity><|><target_entity><|><relationship_description><|><relationship_strength>)

Use ## between records. When finished output <|COMPLETE|>.

-Example-
Entity_types: document,clause,party,permission,prohibition,exception,license_model
Text:
[DOCUMENT] Example EULA
[SECTION] 3.3
[SCOPE] General
[CLAUSE_TEXT] Unless prohibited by an applicable schedule, Licensee may
allocate licences to Affiliates.

[DOCUMENT] Product Schedule
[SECTION] 15.A(ii)
[SCOPE] Transaction Licence
[CLAUSE_TEXT] The licences may not be allocated to any other party.

Output:
("entity"<|>EXAMPLE EULA SECTION 3.3<|>permission<|>Licensee MAY allocate licences to Affiliates in the General scope, CONDITIONED_ON there being no prohibition in an applicable schedule; source Example EULA section 3.3)
##
("entity"<|>PRODUCT SCHEDULE SECTION 15.A(ii)<|>prohibition<|>Licensee MUST NOT allocate licences to another party in the Transaction Licence scope; source Product Schedule section 15.A(ii))
##
("relationship"<|>PRODUCT SCHEDULE SECTION 15.A(ii)<|>EXAMPLE EULA SECTION 3.3<|>QUALIFIES: the product-specific prohibition limits the conditional general permission for the Transaction Licence scope<|>10)
<|COMPLETE|>

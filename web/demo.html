Why this exists

Twenty-five years of enterprise software agreements — and they still surprise me.

I spent twenty-five years in enterprise software licensing, reading and negotiating hundreds of software and cloud agreements across the table from procurement, business owners and in-house legal. Their structure is a real problem for everyone at that table: vendors, procurement and software asset managers alike. Rights are spread across master terms, schedules, order forms and addenda; acquisitions bolt inherited paper onto the stack; years of amendments accrete; and the agreements, as written, produce outcomes nobody intended. The License Model Schedule loaded below still carries licence models inherited through an acquisition I sold through — the paper outlived the org chart. Questions like the ones on this page are the ones seven-figure renewals turn on.

This project asks whether that reading can move, over time, to agents — and what a system has to be shaped like to interpret agreement families reliably. Frontier models can essentially do this work today, but contracts are exactly the documents many organisations cannot send to a cloud API, so local, in-house processing is the attractive path. AgreementAtlas is a first pass at that challenge:

What it does today

  • Parses an agreement family deterministically — instruments, clauses, defined terms, licence models, cross-references, precedence ladders — every field traceable to an exact span of source text.
  • Has a local model read each clause into a structured rule (effect · modality · actor · action · object · conditions), kept only when its quoted evidence validates verbatim against the document.
  • Answers questions from graph-retrieved evidence with a local Gemma 4, cited to the clause — and measured against GPT-5 on a twenty-question trap suite, summarised below.

Where it goes next

  • Sturdier deterministic structure: re-splitting clauses that PDF layout fused, reading two-column tables faithfully, and modelling version chains across amended and reissued agreements.
  • Full sentence anatomy: per-field evidence spans for every proposition in a clause — the licensing sentence diagrammed, on demand, for any agreement.
  • Agent workflows: comparing a draft against the installed family, surfacing the unintended outcomes before signature, and scaling from one family to a portfolio.

A live demonstration · runs entirely on one machine · no document text leaves it

“Can our affiliate use this software?”

Search the PDF for “use” and you will not find the answer. Read one document and the answer is incomplete. OpenText’s EULA forbids assigning the licence at §13.5, and permits allocating it to an Affiliate ten pages earlier at §3.3 — while what those people may actually do turns on a licence model defined in a separate schedule. Whether you can depends on what you mean by “use”, and you only see that by reading all of it together. This project combines deterministic parsing with an AI-assisted knowledge graph so the answer accounts for every document at once.

    Measured, not asserted

    Gemma 4 on a MacBook Pro matches a frontier model on the traps — given the graph.

    Answering modelRuns onTrap scoreWall clock
    Gemma 4 localthis MacBook Pro (M5 Pro)26 / 2795 s
    GPT-5 cloudOpenAI API27 / 27318 s

    Twenty trap questions over four published agreement families — negation, variant ambiguity, false premises, precedence invented where none exists, questions the corpus cannot answer — scored mechanically on how the answer must open, what it must name and what it must not claim, then read by hand. Both models received the same graph-retrieved evidence, so this measures the answering model alone. Across three runs the two stayed within one another's run-to-run variance; the local model answered in about a third of the time with no document text leaving the machine. The sharper finding: with the graph's structured clause readings withheld, the local model's misses turned into confidently wrong answers — once inverting a permissions question — while with them its only misses were explicit refusals. The readings do not change the score so much as the failure mode, and a confident wrong answer is the one failure a licensing tool cannot afford.

    Honest limits

    This is a portfolio demonstration built to understand the problem, not a product. Deterministic labels on unusual sentence forms are still wrong on messy PDF conversions — which is why the answer path reads full quoted clauses, never labels alone, and why every conclusion links to the text that decides it.
